## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> Clearhollow Snow Co. is a two-truck, owner-operated snow removal company serving homeowners and small commercial properties in Bloomington, Edina, and Richfield, Minnesota, seeking a $100,000 SBA 7(a) loan. The plan rests on one idea: a snow business can't control the weather, so it controls how it gets paid. Seasonal contracts pay whatever falls, storm caps bill the extra events in a heavy winter, and per-push and deicing work adds upside when it snows, so the business can pay its loan in a 20-inch winter and keep its promises in a 90-inch one.

### Key Messages

1. **Seasonal contracts carry the weather risk.** Seasonal driveway and commercial plans are 58% of list revenue in a normal winter and are paid in full whatever falls. In Year 1 they alone cover 91% of cash fixed costs including loan payments, rising to 99% by Year 3, before a single per-push visit or deicing application.
2. **Storm caps protect the heavy winters.** Seasonal driveway plans cover up to 20 plowable events and commercial plans up to 28 pushes. In a 90-inch winter like 2022–23, the extra events are billed per push, so heavy snow raises profit ($39,883 in the heavy Year 1 case) instead of eating it through salt and overtime.
3. **Two trucks, tight routes.** Every account sits within about 6 miles of the Bloomington yard. Year 1 runs about 115 driveways and 8 commercial lots per event: commercial lots first (open by 6 AM), then residential routes in a fixed order, about 9.4 hours per truck. Growth comes from route density and, in Year 3, a subcontract plow truck at $85 an hour rather than a third $40,000 truck.
4. **Cash is planned around the calendar.** Costs run 12 months; revenue arrives September to May. A $22,750 reserve carries July–August, when month-end cash bottoms out at $9,923, until contract deposits arrive in September and October. The January 15 installment is the year's biggest cash month.
5. **Honest, lender-ready numbers.** In a normal winter: pre-tax profit of $2,480 in Year 1 (after $10,450 of one-time launch costs), $23,072 in Year 2, and $24,621 in Year 3; debt coverage 1.75× / 2.70× / 2.71×. A mild first winter (20–30 inches) loses $12,848 but still covers the loan payments (1.01× as reported, 1.52× before launch costs) and ends the year with $36,549 in cash.

## Why This Matters

- **Snow swings hard from year to year, and lenders know it.** The Twin Cities had 92.8 inches near MSP Airport in 2022–23, then 20.8 inches the next winter, then 28.2 and 55.3 ([MnDOT winter maintenance report](https://www.dot.state.mn.us/maintenance/winter-report.html)). A snow plan with one forecast and no weather test won't get far with a bank. This sample shows all three winters.
- **Published snow plans skip the money that matters.** The free samples and guides online give per-push price lists and a budget, but no balance sheet, no cash flow, and no answer to "what if it doesn't snow?" One sample multiplies monthly revenue by 12 for a business that bills five months a year.
- **Readers' real questions are about contracts, capacity, and cash.** New snow operators want to know how to price a seasonal contract, how many driveways one truck can clear in a storm, what insurance actually costs, and how to get through the summer with a truck payment and no revenue. This plan answers each with one set of numbers.
- **The timing fits the search.** Snow contracts are sold August through October, and loans for trucks and plows are approved in spring and summer. People write snow removal plans in the off-season, which is when this page will rank and be read.
- **The Twin Cities south metro is a realistic market.** Bloomington, Edina, and Richfield have about 43,500 detached single-family homes, 62–72% owner-occupied, and snow that stays on the ground all winter, so every event needs plowing and deicing.
