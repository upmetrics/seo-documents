## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> A one-van carpet cleaning business makes its money in van-hours, not just jobs. Loomridge Carpet Care shows how per-room pricing with a $169 minimum, a tight service area booked by zone, and repeat homeowners and property managers let one owner-operated van grow from 1.9 to 3.3 day jobs a day, pay the owner every year, and repay an SBA loan.

### Key Messages

1. **Price per room, with a floor.** $55 a room and a $169 minimum keep every visit worth the hour it takes. Without the minimum, a 2-room job leaves $52.52 per van-hour; with it, $82.44.
2. **Drive time is a cost, so the service area is a business decision.** Two jobs priced $245 and $265 leave $84.45 and $58.25 per van-hour because one sits 12 minutes away and the other 45. Loomridge books by zone and charges $35 outside it.
3. **The first year is slow, and the plan says so.** 548 jobs and a $27,459 pre-tax loss in Year 1, with Darnell still paid $45,000. A $27,900 reserve covers the gap; cash bottoms at $14,449 in July 2027.
4. **Repeat business does the growing.** 33% of Year 2 households and 39% of Year 3 households are returning customers, helped by the 12- to 18-month cleaning interval carpet makers recommend. Property managers send batches of units: 9, then 14, then 17 accounts.
5. **One van until the numbers say two.** A helper in Year 2 and a technician in Year 3 raise the daily ceiling without a second truck-mount; commercial work runs in the evening. A second van waits until daily jobs pass about 4.

### What to Borrow / What to Avoid

**Borrow:**
- Reference 1's units of thought: revenue per van day, route density, and the residential/commercial mix. Loomridge shows the inputs Reference 1 hides.
- Reference 2's chapter order and its point about choosing between independence and a franchise (one line in the Company Overview: Darnell chose independence to set his own prices and keep his margin).

**Avoid:**
- Hidden inputs (Reference 1). Every revenue line traces to jobs × price.

- Old or unsourced industry figures (References 2 and 3), valuations, and survival haircuts.
- Vendor promotion and second-person advice inside the plan.

### Writing Tone

**For Sample Business Plan:**
- Write the way the business-plan-template-generator rules say: plain, warm, and written by an experienced owner-operator. Use "we" or "Loomridge," whichever reads more naturally; the introduction speaks as "we" (Upmetrics), never "I."
- Active voice. Back every claim with a number, date, place, or named detail from Section 1B and Section 6B.
- Use the pre-calculated tables in Section 6B exactly as given. Do not invent new numbers.
- Use the trade's words, explained once where a lender might not know them: truck-mount, hot-water extraction, pre-spray, agitation, counter-rotating brush (CRB), wand, hose run, air movers, dry time, spotting, pet urine treatment, protector, move-out clean (turnover), per-room price, minimum charge, trip charge, service zone, van-hour.
- Grade 8–9 reading level, one idea per sentence. Open each H2 plainly with one to three sentences that name Loomridge and the section's focus ("Loomridge Carpet Care prices by the room, with a $169 minimum per visit."). No clever openers, no slogans.
- Mix bullets and prose: lead-in plus bullets for parallel items (customer groups, channels, equipment, vendors, licenses), prose for reasons. Lead into every non-financial visual with a bridge line.
- Never preview a section ("Below, we look at…", "This section covers…").
- Never write "According to [publisher]…" or name the data publisher in the text; the link on the figure is the citation. The Census Bureau may be named, and so may Shaw (a well-known Georgia carpet maker) where its name helps.
- Never name a real metro Atlanta carpet cleaning company, franchise brand, property-management company, or equipment dealer. Real places (I-285, Tucker, Decatur, Marietta, Northlake) are fine as places.

## 9. FAQ Section

> 4 FAQs. None of these questions is answered in the plan chapters above.

**Q1:** How much does it cost to start a carpet cleaning business?
**A1:** Say that this sample budgets $120,000 ($90,000 SBA 7(a) loan + $30,000 owner equity), with the used van and new truck-mount ($66,300) as the largest items and $27,900 kept as cash for the slow first months. Note that a solo operator with a portable machine and an existing vehicle can start for a small fraction of that, but will clean fewer, slower jobs, and that the cost new owners miss is paying themselves and the loan while bookings build. Point readers who are planning a house-cleaning or janitorial business instead to [starting a cleaning business](https://upmetrics.co/blog/how-to-start-cleaning-company-business).
**Source:** Section 6B + Reference 3 (startup ranges) + editorial judgment
**Why not in main content:** The plan states Loomridge's costs; this answers the general question readers bring from search.

**Q2:** How much should I charge for carpet cleaning, and do I need a minimum?
**A2:** Say that many residential carpet cleaners price by the room, with a minimum charge per visit, while commercial work is usually priced by the square foot. Explain how to set a minimum from setup time: if setup and pack-up take about 45 minutes on every job, a small job at the room price earns far less per hour than a full house (in this sample, a 2-room job leaves $52.52 per van-hour without the $169 minimum and $82.44 with it). Advise checking three local competitors' prices and what their quotes include before setting yours.
**Source:** Section 6B (job comparison) + Reference 3 (pricing methods) + editorial judgment
**Why not in main content:** The plan gives Loomridge's prices; this explains how readers can set their own.

**Q3:** Should I buy a truck-mount or start with a portable machine?
**A3:** Say that a truck-mount costs far more and needs a van, but it heats water and pulls water out faster, so carpets dry sooner and each job takes less time; a portable machine costs less and reaches high-rise units and places a hose can't, but jobs take longer. Many owners start with one and add the other: Loomridge buys both, using the portable for condos and apartments beyond hose reach. Advise choosing equipment certified under the Carpet and Rug Institute's Seal of Approval program, since some carpet warranties ask for it.
**Source:** Reference 1 (equipment) + Shaw care guide + editorial judgment
**Why not in main content:** The plan lists Loomridge's equipment; this compares the two choices for readers.

**Q4:** How do I adapt this sample plan for my own carpet cleaning business?
**A4:** Tell readers to change five inputs first: their own price list and minimum, their service zone and drive times, how many jobs they can do a day alone and with help, how many property managers they can sign, and what they need to pay themselves. Then rebuild the financial tables from jobs × average price. Point out that the structure (revenue by service line, a job-time comparison, a slow first year with a cash reserve, and a monthly cash view) stays the same. For a broader cleaning business, point to the [cleaning company business plan](https://upmetrics.co/template/cleaning-company-business-plan) sample.
**Source:** Editorial judgment
**Why not in main content:** The plan is written about Loomridge; adaptation advice belongs outside it.
