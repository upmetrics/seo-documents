## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> The Corner Room seeks a $300,000 SBA loan to open a neighborhood bar in Denver's Berkeley neighborhood that engineers steady Sunday–Thursday demand — not just weekend peaks — so the business is built on repeat local traffic and stays profitable across the whole week.

### Key Messages

1. A bar that only wins on Friday and Saturday is fragile: rent, payroll, and utilities run seven days a week, so weekday demand decides whether a bar survives.
2. Repeat local traffic — regulars who return on weeknights — is a more durable revenue base than weekend or one-time visitors, and it can be built on purpose.
3. Specific weekday levers (a real happy-hour structure, trivia and live-music nights, community partnerships, and a loyalty program) turn slow nights into predictable revenue.
4. The plan is fundable because the numbers are conservative and complete: startup costs, a 3-year P&L, cash flow, balance sheets, and a break-even that a lender can check.
5. The Corner Room breaks even at about $800,000 in annual sales — a figure weekend nights alone cannot reach, which is exactly why the weekday model is the core of the plan, not an add-on.

## 8. Content Outline

> Informed by reference analysis and editorial judgment. Every chapter advances the thesis: a neighborhood bar built for repeat local traffic and weekday demand. Write for The Corner Room specifically — never generic.

### Writing Tone

**For Sample Business Plan:**
- Write like a business consultant drafting a formal plan for a client.
- Third person throughout — "The Corner Room will..." not "We will..."
- Back every claim with a specific number, date, or named detail.
- Financial sections use the pre-calculated tables in Section 6B — do not invent new numbers.
- Replace vague language with specifics: "12% revenue growth in Year 2" not "significant growth."
- Use bar-industry vocabulary: covers, average check, pour cost, dayparts, happy hour, comps, front-of-house/back-of-house, RevPASH where useful.
- Keep it readable (Grade 8–9), short paragraphs, "you" only in FAQs — the plan body stays third person.
- NO banned AI terms (see Appendix).

### Section Priority Labels

- 🔴 **MUST HAVE** — essential to the plan or thesis.
- 🟡 **SHOULD HAVE** — important for depth.
