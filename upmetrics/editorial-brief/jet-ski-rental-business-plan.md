## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> A four-ski jet ski rental can repay its loan when the owner gets legal dock space before buying a single ski, prices for the 130 or so days a year when tourists ride, and plans the cash for the five months when nobody does. Bramblewake Jet Ski Rentals shows how a first-time owner on Table Rock Lake moves from a planned first-year loss to debt coverage of 1.45× in Year 2, with thin margins shown honestly and a plan for a bad summer.

### Key Messages

1. **The dock comes before the skis.** Table Rock Lake is a Corps of Engineers lake: private docks can't be rented out, and doing business on the water needs written permission. Bramblewake's dock is a sublicense from a concession marina that pays the marina 10% of gross receipts, and it was signed before the loan application.
2. **The season is short and the summer is everything.** Bramblewake is open 131 days in Year 1. July alone brings 33% of Year 1 revenue, and June–August 82%.
3. **The off-season is a cost line, not a pause.** From November to March the business earns nothing and still pays $19,749, more than half of it loan payments. The plan covers it with a reserve, an owner who takes no off-season salary, and an insurance renewal paid in installments.
4. **The margins are thin, and the plan says so.** Revenue $161,319 → $204,406 → $227,404; a −$28,663 pre-tax loss in Year 1 (including $19,200 of one-time costs), then $13,497 and $23,463. Debt coverage is 1.45× in Year 2, and a summer 3.7% weaker would push it below 1.25×, so the plan names the levers Caleb pulls if it happens.
5. **Safety is the business.** Personal watercraft were 19% of the vessels in U.S. boating accidents in 2024, and almost a third of those jet skis were rented. Every Bramblewake rule (age limits, boater cards, a ride zone with GPS alerts, a support ski on the water) is there to keep a first-time rider safe and the insurance in force.

## Why This Matters

- **Jet ski plans online skip the water.** The sample plans and guides we found list startup cost ranges and generic marketing ideas, but none explains how a rental business gets legal dock space, and none shows what a winter with no revenue does to cash. Readers searching for a jet ski rental business plan want those answers for a real lake.
- **It answers a first-time owner's real questions:** what four skis really cost once freight, prep, and tax are added; what to charge for an hour against the marina down the shore; how many attendants a dock needs; what the insurance and boater-card rules require; and how much cash has to be in the bank on November 1.
- **It shows lenders what they worry about.** A plan that admits a Year 1 loss, shows a stress test for a wet summer, and sizes the reserve for the off-season reads as more credible than one that assumes full skis every afternoon.
