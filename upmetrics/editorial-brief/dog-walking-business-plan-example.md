## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> A small, owner-operated dog walking business wins not by charging the least, but by owning a tight service area — dense routes, recurring weekday clients, and relentless communication turn a one-person operation into a predictable, profitable business and set a clear financial line for when to hire.

### Key Messages

1. Route density, not headcount, is the core profit driver — five clients in one tight zone beat eight clients scattered across a city.
2. Recurring weekday clients create predictable revenue, easier route planning, and lower admin, which is why they anchor the entire model.
3. A solo walker's revenue is capped by peak-hour (midday) capacity, so capacity management is a financial decision, not just a scheduling one.
4. There is a specific, calculable point — roughly full solo capacity — where hiring a second walker becomes financially practical rather than premature.
5. Trust and communication are the product. They justify premium pricing and drive the client retention that makes recurring revenue work.
