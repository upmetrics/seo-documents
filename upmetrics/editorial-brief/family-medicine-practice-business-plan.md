## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> Northbrook Family Medicine, PLLC seeks $245,000 in SBA 7(a) financing to open a lean, physician-owned family medicine practice in Haywood Springs, NC — reaching cash-flow break-even in Year 2 by deliberately building a patient panel, managing payer mix, and scaling staff only as visit demand justifies it.

### Key Messages

1. An independent family medicine practice is a cash-flow business before it is a profit business — the first year runs at a planned loss while the panel fills and the first insurance reimbursements clear.
2. Panel size, payer mix, and provider utilization — not vanity revenue — are the three levers that decide whether a solo practice survives its first 24 months.
3. Reimbursement timing (not just reimbursement rate) is a founder-level risk: a 40-day collection lag means a profitable practice can still run short on cash without a working-capital reserve.
4. Hiring a second clinician is a math decision triggered by sustained visit demand near physician capacity — not an ambition or a calendar milestone.
5. A lender-ready plan for a medical practice has to model the clinical realities — credentialing lag, overhead ratios, same-day slots — that generic templates skip.

## 8. Content Outline

> Informed by reference analysis and editorial judgment. Every chapter uses family-medicine terminology and the Northbrook business profile. The information-gain chapters are what separate this from a fill-in-the-blank template.

### Writing Tone

**For Sample Business Plan:**
- Write like a professional consultant drafting a formal plan for a client; third person throughout ("Northbrook Family Medicine will…", "Dr. Donovan will…").
- Every claim backed by a specific number, date, or named detail. Replace vague language with specifics ("25% year-over-year visit growth," not "significant growth").
- Financial sections use the pre-calculated tables from Section 6B verbatim — do not invent new numbers.
- Use the industry's vocabulary: panel, payer mix, E/M codes, DSO/days in A/R, credentialing, provider utilization, same-day access, overhead ratio.
- The information-gain chapters may shift to an explanatory register (teaching the strategic reality) while staying in third person and tied to Northbrook's numbers.

**General rules:** Grade 8–9 reading level; short paragraphs; real numbers; no filler, no passive voice, no AI buzzwords (see Appendix banned list).

### Section Priority Labels

- 🔴 **MUST HAVE** — essential to the plan or the thesis.
- 🟡 **SHOULD HAVE** — important for depth.
- 🟢 **NICE TO HAVE** — differentiation; deprioritize if over length.

> ~500 words, 4-6 paragraphs | 🔴 MUST HAVE (information-gain)

> 🔗 Transition from previous: "Knowing who the patients are is one thing; understanding how slowly — and how predictably — a panel actually fills is what keeps a new practice solvent."
> 🚫 Avoid: Turning this into a motivational pep talk. Keep it quantitative and tied to Northbrook's numbers.
> 📖 Reference: None (original analysis; cite external source 1).

Explain: what a panel is; benchmark panel sizes (cite JABFM — avg ~1,933, norm ~2,500, independent practices larger); how panel size maps to visits (visits per patient per year); how Northbrook's visit ramp (3,600 → 4,500 → 5,625) reflects a filling panel; and how revenue derives from visits × $135 collection. Connect to the revenue forecast. Make the point that the Year 1 loss is a panel-timing problem, not a demand problem.
