## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> Whitfield Learning Studio seeks a $30,000 SBA Microloan to move a 17-student home tutoring practice into a 1,000 sq ft studio in Ashbury Glen, Pennsylvania. The plan shows that the first 39 student-sessions each month pay the rent, that Year 1 roughly breaks even after $6,500 of launch costs while paying Lauren a $45,000 salary, and that the studio uses only 57% of its teaching capacity by Year 3, so growth depends on hiring tutors, not on finding more space.

### Key Messages

1. **The rent is covered by enrollment, not hope.** Rent is $21,000 in Year 1 ($1,750 a month). Each student-session contributes $45.25 after materials, payment fees and tutor pay, so 39 sessions a month cover the rent. Rent falls from 16.5% of revenue in Year 1 to 12.2% in Year 3.
2. **Small groups are what make a rented room pay.** A 1:1 hour earns $75. A group of three earns $135 in the same hour with the same tutor. 45% of student-sessions are group seats, which is why the studio's blended revenue per teaching hour is about $86.
3. **Recurring enrollment smooths a seasonal business.** Monthly autopay plans, 12-week re-assessments and monthly progress reports keep students enrolled. Demand still dips in December and over the summer, and the Year 1 monthly cash table shows the studio never drops below $15,020 in cash.
4. **The founder is paid as an employee from day one.** Lauren's salary ($45,000, $52,000, $58,000) sits in operating expenses. After Social Security and Medicare, Year 1 pays her about $41,600, slightly more than the ~$40,400 she keeps today after self-employment tax. By Year 3 she earns $58,000 plus $16,818 of pre-tax profit stays in the business.
5. **Growth is gated by tutors, and tutors are paid fairly.** Part-time tutors earn $27 an hour ($28 in Year 3), above the national median for tutors. A new tutor is added only when Lauren's schedule and the waitlist both justify it.

## Why This Matters

- The page at this URL dates from 2019 and needs a full replacement. People searching "tutoring business plan" want a filled-in example with numbers they can check and adapt.
- Demand is real and local. The 2024 Nation's Report Card found about 40% of 4th graders below Basic in reading and nearly 40% of 8th graders below Basic in math. Meanwhile, the share of public schools offering tutoring fell from 51% to 43% between 2022 and 2025, which sends more parents to paid options.
- Most sample tutoring plans fall into two camps: home-tutor side gigs with no rent or staff, or franchise-scale centers with six-figure budgets and venture-style growth. The step this plan covers, moving an existing practice into rented rooms, is the one many real tutors face and few samples address.
- Tutors are first-time founders applying for small loans (SBA Microloans, community lenders), which puts them in Upmetrics' core audience of people preparing lender-ready plans.

### What to Borrow / What to Avoid

**Borrow:**
- Reference 1's location checklist and the high-dosage tutoring design (frequent sessions, consistent tutor, small groups). These shape Whitfield's 1–2 sessions a week and the 4-student group cap.
- Reference 2's group pricing logic (each group seat priced below 1:1, more per hour in total). Whitfield's $45 seat is 60% of the $75 1:1 rate.
- Reference 3's chapter order and its idea of assessments and progress reports as services. Whitfield turns them into a priced diagnostic, a monthly report and 12-week re-assessments.

**Avoid:**
- Every reference's lack of a capacity model. Whitfield's plan counts teaching stations, slots and tutor hours, and ties them to rent.
- Reference 3's numbers that aren't built from anything. Every Whitfield revenue figure is sessions × price.
- Reference 1's franchise tilt. Mention the franchise option once, in the Competitive Analysis, and move on.
- How-to explanations in general. This is a finished plan, written in the third person.
