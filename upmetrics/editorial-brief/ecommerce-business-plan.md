## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> Shelfwren Home Co. is asking for a $75,000 SBA 7(a) loan to launch an online home organization store from its founder's basement in Columbus, Ohio. Starting at home keeps fixed costs low, so the loan goes where an online store's money actually goes: inventory bought months ahead, and traffic bought before repeat customers arrive. The plan proves, traffic source by traffic source, that visits become orders and orders leave money after product, shipping, fees, and ads.

### Key Messages

1. **The plan is built from traffic, not a revenue target.** Year 1: about 240,000 website sessions at a 1.5% conversion rate = 3,600 orders × $72.00 = $259,200. Each traffic source has its own sessions, conversion rate, and ad cost, and the total ties to the P&L.
2. **Not all traffic is profitable on the first order, and the plan says so.** Paid social costs $40.91 in ads per order against $35.50 of contribution before ads, a loss of about $5 per first order that repeat purchases repay. Google Shopping covers its cost on the first order. Email earns $3.24 per session and organic Pinterest and search bring 872 orders with no ad cost.
3. **Private label is where the margin is.** Private-label products cost 27–30% of their selling price landed in Columbus; wholesale lines cost 34.5–35%. A 65/35 mix gives a 69.5% product margin before packaging, and a 62.6% gross margin in Year 1.
4. **Home first, 3PL second.** The basement handles Year 1's 300 orders a month at no rent. When volume reaches 390 a month in Year 2, a Columbus-area 3PL takes over storage and packing for about $4.30 an order, so growth isn't capped by Columbus's 20%-of-home limit or by evenings at the packing table.
5. **The numbers are honest about Year 1 and about cash.** Year 1 shows a $17,641 pre-tax loss because it absorbs $40,100 of one-time launch costs; before them, it earns $22,459. Profit follows in Year 2 ($34,346) and Year 3 ($45,164), with debt service coverage of 3.59× and 4.43×. Month-end cash never falls below $24,562.

## Why This Matters

- **The current Upmetrics page explains, but doesn't show.** /e-commerce-business-plan-example is a 2019 how-to guide with no finished plan. Searchers looking for an "example" leave without one. This sample gives them a complete plan at the same URL.
- **Online buying keeps growing, and small stores pay for it.** E-commerce reached 17.1% of U.S. retail sales in the second quarter of 2026, up from 16.3% a year earlier ([FRED / U.S. Census Bureau](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/ECOMPCTSA)). Most of a new store's visitors still have to be bought, so a plan that skips traffic costs isn't credible.
- **Published e-commerce sample plans don't show the funnel.** The references below either skip unit economics entirely, show financials only as images, or describe a store with no traffic numbers. None ties sessions, conversion rate, ad cost, and contribution to a P&L.
- **Home-based founders are the real audience.** Most first-time online sellers start from a spare room or basement. Few plans explain local home-business rules, what a $75,000 loan pays for when there's no lease, or when to move to a 3PL.
- **Import costs changed in 2026.** Tariff rules shifted several times this year and duty-free treatment for small shipments remains suspended, so landed cost has to be checked order by order. A plan that shows landed cost, not just factory price, holds up better with a lender.
