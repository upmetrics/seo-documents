## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> A small rage room makes money when its supply of smashables stays cheap and steady, every booking is priced for its true cost, and its weekend rooms fill with dates, birthdays, and groups after the first-visit curiosity fades. Crashwick Rage Room shows how four rooms, a weekly thrift-and-cleanout pickup route, package prices built on room time, and occasion marketing take a first-time owner in Frisco from a planned first-year loss to steady profit, while paying the owner every year and repaying an SBA loan.

### Key Messages

1. **Supply is a weekly job, not a one-time purchase.** Crashwick needs about 2,152 glass and ceramic items a week in Year 1 (2,789 by Year 3). Five sources, led by thrift store partners at about $0.44 an item, keep the blended cost at $0.35; smashables are 14.5% of revenue and disposal 5.2%.
2. **Solo sessions don't carry the business.** After glass, disposal, gear, fees, staff, and their share of room time, a Quick Break loses −$8.64 and a Full Wreck −$6.36, while a Date Night leaves $31.40 and a Birthday Party $138.90.
3. **Saturday evening is the pinch point.** Standard rooms are 68% booked Saturday 5–11 PM in Year 1 and 79% by Year 3, while Wednesday and Thursday stay under a quarter booked. Saturday evenings go to dates and groups first; Thursday Date Night and weekday team events fill the quiet days.
4. **Novelty fades, and the plan says so.** First-time bookings fall from 3,165 to 2,848 by Year 3, but returning guests grow from 14% to 35% of bookings and revenue still grows 19.8% and 16.6%.
5. **The first year is tight but covered.** A −$30,248 pre-tax loss in Year 1 (with $27,100 of one-time launch costs and Renee paid $42,000), a $38,900 reserve, and a cash low of $27,886 in May 2027.

## Why This Matters

- **The rage room samples online skip the hard parts.** The plans we found give revenue without session costs, say nothing about where the glass comes from or where it goes, and one shows a budget that doesn't add up. Readers searching for a rage room business plan want to know what one session really costs and earns.
- **It answers a first-time owner's real questions:** where to get things to break every week and what they cost, what to do with the broken glass and electronics, how many bookings four rooms can hold on a weekend, what to charge for a date or a party, and how the business keeps growing after everyone has tried it once.
- **It shows lenders the risk they worry about most.** A plan that admits first-time bookings will fall, and shows what replaces them, reads as more credible than one with steady growth from novelty alone.
- **It shows what Upmetrics does.** The chain from bookings and packages to three statements and a monthly cash low is what the Upmetrics financial forecasting tool builds, so the product tips fit naturally.

- Budgets that don't add up and revenue without volumes (all three). Every Crashwick number traces to bookings, package contents, and room time.
