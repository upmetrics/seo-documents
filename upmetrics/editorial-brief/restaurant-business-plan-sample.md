## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> A restaurant business plan earns funding only when it proves the concept works as a business — when location, customers, menu, pricing, seating, staffing, and repeat demand all connect to financial assumptions a lender can defend — so this sample shows every one of those links in a real, numbers-backed example.

### Key Messages

1. A plan's job is proof, not description: the reader should finish able to say "this restaurant can realistically hit $1.18M in Year 1 and service its debt," because every number traces back to a stated operating decision.
2. Revenue in a restaurant is not a guess — it is seats × turns × average check × operating days, and each of those inputs is a deliberate, defensible choice.
3. The two numbers that decide a restaurant's survival are prime cost (food + labor) and repeat demand; this plan holds both to specific targets (≈61% prime cost, repeat-visit-driven growth).
4. Most "restaurant failure" fear is overstated — the real three-year failure rate is closer to 60%, not 90% — and the difference between surviving and failing is usually capitalization and unit economics, both of which this plan addresses head-on.
5. A credible plan names its risks (food inflation, labor, slow ramp, seasonality, lease, undercapitalization) and shows the specific buffer or decision that absorbs each one.

## 8. Content Outline

> Informed by the two reference articles' structure and by what a lender actually scrutinizes. Every section advances the thesis: *prove the concept works by connecting decisions to numbers.*

### Writing Tone

**For Sample Business Plan:**
- Write like a professional business consultant drafting a formal plan for a client.
- Third-person throughout — "Heritage Kitchen will..." not "We will..." or "I will..."
- Every claim backed by a specific number, date, or named detail.
- Financial sections use the actual pre-calculated tables from Section 6B — do NOT invent new numbers.
- Replace vague language with specifics: "26.0% revenue growth driven by higher table turns" not "significant growth."
- Use restaurant vocabulary: covers, table turns, average check, prime cost, food cost %, front-of-house (FOH), back-of-house (BOH), seat, cover count.
- The plan should read as if it could be submitted to Summit Peak Bank tomorrow.
- Keep the fictional business name, owner names, location, and figures consistent throughout.

**General rules (all types):**
- Target reading level: Grade 8–9.
- Plain language; short paragraphs (3–4 sentences) except where a table or financial narrative needs more.
- Real numbers and specifics everywhere; no filler, no passive voice, no AI buzzwords.
- See BANNED AI TERMS in the Appendix.

### Section Priority Labels

- 🔴 **MUST HAVE** — essential to the plan or the thesis.
- 🟡 **SHOULD HAVE** — important depth.
- 🟢 **NICE TO HAVE** — differentiation, droppable if long.
