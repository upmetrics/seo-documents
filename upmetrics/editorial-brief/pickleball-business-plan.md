## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> Dinkwell Pickleball Club is asking for a $400,000 SBA 7(a) loan to open a six-court indoor club in south Naperville. It won't win on the sport's growth story or on the most courts. It wins on two things players in Naperville say they can't count on: a booked court that is ready on time, and a game with players at their own level. The financial plan sells only 37.8% of the club's court-hours in Year 1 and still covers its costs before one-time opening expenses.

### Key Messages

1. **The plan runs on court-hours, not on the sport's growth.** Six courts open 6 AM–10 PM on weekdays and 7 AM–9 PM on weekends give 33,312 court-hours a year. Dinkwell books 12,600 of them in Year 1 at an average of $55.50, then 10% more each year at the same prices. Break-even is 11,429 court-hours a year (34% utilization) at steady-state costs.
2. **Reliable bookings and skill matching are the advantage.** Members book up to 8 days ahead, prime-time bookings are capped so no group holds the best courts, no-shows pay a fee, and the Dinkwell Court Promise gives a free hour if a booked court isn't ready on time. Open play, leagues and clinics run in five Dinkwell Levels, so players get games at their own level.
3. **Memberships grow slowly by design.** Average paying members are 175, 190 and 205 over the three years, with dues about 25% of revenue. Leagues, clinics and lessons grow from 32% to 35% of revenue, so the plan doesn't depend on members alone.
4. **Winter fills the courts and summer empties them, and the cash plan is built around that.** Prime-time utilization runs about 78% in January and 39% in July. Opening on October 1 lets the first winter build a cash cushion before the first summer. Month-end cash never falls below $90,947.
5. **Honest numbers a lender can check.** Rent is $187,500 in Year 1, 27% of revenue. Year 1 shows a pre-tax loss of $25,928 after $53,500 of one-time opening costs, then a profit of $18,904 in Year 2 (when a salaried assistant manager joins) and $59,467 in Year 3. Debt coverage is 0.82× in Year 1 as reported (1.67× before one-time costs), 1.50× in Year 2 and 2.10× in Year 3.

### What to Borrow / What to Avoid

**Borrow:**
- Reference 1's monthly cost check: Dinkwell's Year 1 rent of $15,625 a month and payroll near $20,000 a month (staff and owner, with payroll tax) sit in a believable range for a six-court staffed club.
- Reference 1's staffed vs. autonomous idea, used narrowly: Dinkwell is staffed, with members-only app entry from 6–8 AM on weekdays.
- Reference 2's membership tiers and its point that strong clubs earn a large share outside dues (Dinkwell: about three-quarters).
- Reference 3's split of one-time startup costs from ongoing operating costs.

**Avoid:**
- Revenue with no capacity behind it. Ours is court-hours × average revenue, checked against 33,312 available court-hours, prime and off-peak.
- Unsourced player counts. Use the SFIA figure and the Naperville Census figures only.
- "Profitable within 12–18 months" claims. Dinkwell loses money in Year 1 and says so.
- Ignoring HVAC, lighting and the lease. These are the largest build-out and operating costs, and our plan names them.
- A big management team. Dinkwell is one owner, part-time desk staff and coaches, and one assistant manager from Year 2.
- Second-person advice inside the plan chapters.
