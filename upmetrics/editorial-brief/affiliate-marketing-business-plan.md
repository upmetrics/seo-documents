### Owner Bio

> **Marisol Bennett (41), founder,** spent 11 years at a Triangle-area sewing and vacuum dealer (do not name it), first as a service technician and then as the lead class instructor. ## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> An affiliate review business is only as strong as the trust behind its tests and the timing of its cash. MakerBench Reviews shows a lender two years of real commissions, the cost and payback of each review, and a plan to spread its income beyond Amazon, so an $18,000 microloan with no new owner money is repaid from earnings even if commissions fall or merchants pay late.

### Key Messages

1. **Proven earnings, kept apart from the forecast.** $31,900 in 2025 and about $51,613 in 2026, with clicks, orders, reversals, and Amazon's share shown year by year. Projections start from those numbers: $70,044, $84,770, $97,130.
2. **Each review is a small investment with a known payback.** A cutting-machine review costs $519.50 in cash and needs about 26 machine sales to repay it, about 9 months at 3 sales a month. A sewing-machine review costs $619.50 and repays in about 11 months.
3. **Less Amazon, more direct programs.** Amazon pays 3.5% on average but converts 8% of clicks; brand and dealer programs pay 6–8% on $310–$560 orders. Amazon falls from 50% of commissions in 2026 to 41% in 2027 and 33% in 2029.
4. **Money arrives two months late, and the plan holds cash for it.** About 88 days of revenue is always owed at year-end. Month-end cash in 2027 never falls below $14,463.
5. **The loan survives bad news.** A 25% commission cut from April 2027 plus 30-day payout delays still leaves cash above $3,270 with no changes, and above $10,999 once Marisol trims costs. The $386.89 payment is made in every case.

## 7. Recommended Word Count

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Target | 6,000 — 6,800 words including tables |
| Rationale | An affiliate plan has to explain things a lender rarely sees (merchant clicks, conversion rates, commission reversals, payouts that arrive two months late), and it carries two years of history next to three years of projections. Revenue Model and Operations need more room than in a store plan. The information-gain H2 ("What One Review Costs and Earns") takes about 800 words. The financial tables carry the numbers, so the Financial Plan prose stays short. |

## 8. Content Outline

> Built from the reference analysis and editorial judgment. Every chapter supports the thesis: MakerBench's income rests on trusted, hands-on tests and on cash that arrives two months after each sale, and the plan proves the $18,000 microloan is repaid from earnings even if commissions fall or merchants pay late. Write for MakerBench specifically. Never write generic instructions like "describe your audience."

## 9. FAQ Section

> 4 FAQs. None of these questions is answered in the plan chapters above.

**Q1:** How do affiliate marketers get paid?
**A1:** Explain in plain words: a merchant pays a commission when a reader clicks an affiliate link and buys within the cookie window. Commissions are usually a percentage of the sale, sometimes a flat fee. Most programs hold commissions until the return window closes, then pay monthly, often 30–60 days after the month ends, and take back commissions on returned orders. Say that this delay is why an affiliate plan needs a cash buffer.
**Source:** Amazon Associates payment help page + Section 6B + editorial judgment
**Why not in main content:** The plan describes MakerBench's merchants; this answers the general search question.

**Q2:** How long does it take for affiliate marketing to make money?
**A2:** Say that most affiliate sites earn little in the first year, because new pages take months to rank and payouts lag sales by about two months. In this sample, the business earned $1,850 in its launch quarter, $31,900 in its first full year, and about $51,613 in its second. Advise readers to count only paid commissions, not pending ones, when they plan.
**Source:** Section 6B Table 0 + editorial judgment
**Why not in main content:** The plan shows MakerBench's history; this generalizes the timeline.

**Q3:** Can an affiliate marketing business get a small business loan?
**A3:** Say yes, if it has at least a year or two of commission history, a clear use for the money, and statements that show repayment. SBA microloans (up to $50,000, made through nonprofit lenders) suit small online businesses. Point readers to the guide on [SBA loans](https://upmetrics.co/blog/what-is-an-sba-loan) and suggest checking the monthly payment with a [business loan calculator](https://upmetrics.co/ai-tools/business-loan-calculator) before applying. Mention that lenders often ask for merchant payout statements and tax returns.
**Source:** Editorial judgment + SBA microloan program facts (cap $50,000, made through intermediary lenders)
**Why not in main content:** The plan states MakerBench's loan; this answers the general funding question.

**Q4:** How do I adapt this affiliate marketing business plan for my own niche?
**A4:** Tell readers to change five inputs first: their own clicks by channel, conversion rate and commission rate for each merchant, reversal rate, the cost of one piece of content, and what they need to pay themselves. Then rebuild the tables from clicks, orders, and commissions, and rerun the stress test with their largest merchant cut by a quarter. For affiliates whose income is mostly display ads, point to the [blog business plan](https://upmetrics.co/template/blog-business-plan).
