## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> Ninefold Escape Rooms is asking for a $200,000 SBA 7(a) loan to open a three-game escape room in a suburb that doesn't have one. The plan doesn't rest on excitement about escape rooms. It rests on a count of bookings: how many 90-minute slots each room can run, how many of those slots fill on weekdays and weekends, how many players pay in each group and what each player pays. At 1,710 bookings in Year 2 (about 34 a week), the venue covers its operating costs, Tessa's salary and its loan payments 1.66 times, and the cash reserve carries Year 1 while bookings build.

### Key Messages

1. **Revenue is slots × fill rate × group size × price.** Three rooms on a 90-minute cycle give 57 weekend and 36 weekday-evening public slots a week. Year 1 fills 34% of weekend slots and 22% of weekday slots, with 4.5 and 4.0 paying players per group.
2. **Weekends pay the bills; weekdays and corporate events fill the gaps.** Weekend bookings are 72% of Year 1 revenue. Weekday evenings are priced lower to pull groups into quiet slots, and 25 corporate events use daytime hours when the rooms would otherwise sit empty.
3. **Break-even is a number of bookings a week, and the plan clears it.** At Year 2 costs the venue breaks even at 24.5 bookings a week and covers its loan payments too at 30.0 a week. The Year 2 plan is 33.5 a week.
4. **The owner is paid and the games wear out.** Tessa's salary ($45,000 rising to $52,000), game master wages, rising repair costs and a $36,000 reserve to rebuild the first room in Year 4 are all in the numbers.
5. **The loan is sized to what it buys, and Year 1 is funded honestly.** Build-out, three game builds and monitoring total $185,900. Year 1 covers its loan payments only 0.87 times, so the plan keeps a $53,600 cash reserve. Year-end cash never falls below $57,946, even with bookings 15% below plan.

## Why This Matters

- The current page tells readers what to write but never shows a finished plan. A complete, lender-ready example is what "escape room business plan" searchers need, and it matches the new-format samples in the library.
- The U.S. escape room market has stopped growing: about 2,000 venues, flat since 2023, and 48% of all venues ever opened have since closed (Room Escape Artist, December 2025). A lender reading an escape room plan in 2027 wants proof of booking volume, not enthusiasm.
- Most published escape room plans skip the one thing that decides survival: how many slots a room can run and how many of them fill. This sample builds the whole forecast from that, with separate weekday and weekend demand.
- It shows Upmetrics' financial forecasting at work: a driver-based revenue forecast, monthly ramp-up, a COGS build-up, owner pay and a full SBA loan schedule.

- Revenue with no rooms, slots, group size or prices behind it (Reference 3)
