## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> Batch & Bow Cookie Co. is asking for a $40,000 SBA Microloan to add the oven capacity and licensed kitchen its paying customers are already waiting for. It doesn't need a storefront: a small menu, preorders with fixed pickup windows and repeat office orders can pay both founders a salary from Year 1 and repay the loan with room to spare.

### Key Messages

1. **The demand is already paid for, not guessed.** 1,620 paid orders, 58% repeat customers and 41 sold-out weeks show the home kitchen ran out of capacity before it ran out of customers.
2. **Preorders control waste and cash.** Customers pay at checkout, the bake list is fixed by Tuesday night, and there are almost no unsold cookies. This is why the plan works without a storefront.
3. **A focused menu keeps production efficient.** Six core flavors plus one monthly flavor means fewer doughs, simpler purchasing and consistent quality as volume grows.
4. **Office and gift orders smooth out the year.** Office accounts add steady weekday revenue. Gift boxes carry the fourth quarter, which brings in 37% of Year 1 sales.
5. **The numbers are deliberately modest.** Flat prices, 20% yearly growth, founder pay of $26,000 each in Year 1 and no new equipment for three years. Debt service coverage is 1.55x in Year 1 and 3.28x by Year 3.

### What to Borrow / What to Avoid

**Borrow:**
- Reference 1's cost-plus pricing logic, shown with Batch & Bow's real numbers (see the Pricing Strategy writing instructions)
- Reference 2's lender checklist: amount requested + owner contribution in the summary, an ownership % table, supplier payment terms, customer "needs and fears"
- Reference 2's phased growth framing (home → leased space → added capacity), used as the plan's backbone
- Reference 3's sources-and-uses framing for the Startup Costs + Source of Funds pair, and its operations prompts (hours, seasonal changes, key assets, suppliers)
- Swirlwick's "What Supports the Forecast" subsection and its "Loan Repayment & Debt Coverage" table

**Avoid:**
- Generic instructions in place of a worked example (References 1 and 3)
- A storefront assumption: no foot traffic, display cases or walk-in counter sales anywhere in this plan
- Hockey-stick revenue or undercounted labor (Reference 1's 15-minutes-per-dozen labor)
- Recycling Swirlwick's San Diego details, owner names, menu structure or wording
