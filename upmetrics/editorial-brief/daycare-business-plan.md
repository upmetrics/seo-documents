## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> Willowmere Early Learning Center is asking for a $325,000 SBA 7(a) loan to open a 60-place licensed center in Cedar Prairie, Indiana, in a county with only 80 licensed child care places for every 100 young children. The plan holds up because every number comes from Indiana's staffing ratios and a classroom-by-classroom enrollment count. A $172,950 reserve carries the first-year ramp, and from Year 2 the center covers its loan 1.30 times, and 2.04 times by Year 3, after paying both owners.

### Key Messages

1. **Revenue is a count of enrolled children by classroom, not a market-share guess.** Willowmere opens with 24 registered children and reaches 50 of 60 places by May 2028. Year 2 averages 50 children (83.3%) and Year 3 averages 53 (88.3%). Tuition is $310–$410 a week, and the blended average of $1,535 a month sits below the county's $1,625 median for top-rated programs.
2. **Staffing comes from Indiana's ratios, hour by hour.** Infants are 1:4, toddlers and twos 1:5, threes 1:10 and Pre-K 1:12, and the youngest child in a group sets its ratio. The plan counts teachers needed in each block of the 6:30 AM–6:00 PM day, then adds breaks and absences: 510.9 paid teacher hours a week in Year 2, from 14 staff.
3. **The classroom mix is the business model.** Rooms for children under 3 bring in $27–$30 of tuition per teacher hour; the Threes and Pre-K rooms bring in $44–$46. Willowmere keeps 8 infant places to meet local demand and licenses 20 places for 3-year-olds to pay for them.
4. **Break-even is about 45 children, and the plan shows it.** At Year 2 costs, Willowmere breaks even at 45.3 enrolled children (75.5% of capacity). Year 2 plans 50, a cushion of 4.7 children. The stress test shows what happens with fewer children and what the owners do about it.
5. **The owners and the ramp are paid for up front.** Darnell's $60,000 director salary and Luis's reduced $30,000 are expenses before profit. The Year 1 loss of $140,986 (including $53,000 of one-time opening costs) is covered by the reserve. Cash never drops below $91,257.

## Why This Matters

- The current page is a 2019 sample (Kidzvilla) with no state, no ratios and no numbers that tie together. Daycare is one of the most-read business types in the template library, and lenders reject daycare plans that skip ratio-based staffing. A complete, funding-ready example matches the other new-format samples.
- Most published daycare samples quote break-even as a general occupancy range ("60–80%") without showing how ratios create it. This plan shows the classroom economics, the hour-by-hour staffing and the break-even at 45.3 children. Founders can copy that logic.
- Infant and toddler care is the most underserved segment and the hardest to make pay. A plan that is honest about this (8 infant places, priced at $410 a week, carried by larger preschool rooms) is more useful to a founder and more believable to a bank.
- It shows Upmetrics' financial forecasting at work: a driver-based revenue forecast by classroom, monthly ramp-up, payroll built from staffing hours, an SBA loan schedule, debt coverage and a stress test.

## 6B. Financial Projections

> These are the ACTUAL tables the writer must use, as-is, in the Financial Plan chapter and in the Executive Summary, Classrooms, Staffing and Operations chapters where noted. Every number was calculated by script from the assumptions below and passed all 63 validation checks. The checks cover:
> - tuition = enrolled children × weekly tuition × billed weeks, by room and month
> - food and supplies = child-days × cost per day
> - teacher hours from the ratio model
> - COGS, gross profit, EBITDA, EBIT and net income
> - depreciation and the Net PP&E roll-forward
> - retained earnings and the loan roll-forward from the amortization schedule
> - startup costs = loan + equity
> - the balance sheet balances every year, and cash flow ending cash = balance sheet cash
> - monthly Year 1 cash ends at the year-end balance sheet cash
> - enrollment never exceeds licensed capacity
>
> Do NOT edit, round or recalculate any figure.
>
> Accounting notes for the writer:
> 1. The 12-month insurance premium ($16,000) was prepaid before opening, so it is used up in Year 1 and appears in Year 1 insurance.
> 2. The $8,550 lease deposit is refundable, so it stays on the balance sheet every year.
