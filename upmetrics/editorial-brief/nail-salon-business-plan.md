## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> Fernbright Nail Studio is asking for a $110,000 SBA 7(a) loan to open a mid-priced neighborhood nail salon in Ballantyne, Charlotte. A nail salon earns money when its chairs have clients in them, so the plan is built around keeping them full at a steady price: an owner who brings her own regulars, commission pay that keeps labor in step with demand, online booking and rebooking at checkout, and the same quality on every visit.

### Key Messages

1. **The chairs are the inventory.** Fernbright has 10 stations, but on an average hour only 2–3 are in use; Saturdays, weekday evenings, and the weeks before Mother's Day and the holidays run near full. The plan schedules part-time techs for those peaks and keeps tech utilization at 69% in Year 1, 75% in Year 2, and 76% in Year 3.
2. **The owner's own clients carry the first year.** Thao Bennett's 180 regulars fill about 25 chair hours a week, roughly 1,070 of Year 1's 6,400 visits. Her services carry no commission, so they lift Year 1's gross margin to 49.8%.
3. **Commission pay keeps labor in step with demand.** Techs earn 45% of their own sales with a $12/hour floor, about $15.45 an hour in Year 1 and $21.63 with tips. A full-time tech earns about $37,900 with tips in Year 1, above the national median for manicurists.
4. **Repeat visits drive growth, not discounts.** Gel and fill clients return every 2–3 weeks. Rebooking at checkout and Fernbright Standing Appointments push visits up 15% a year at a flat $58 average ticket; discounts stay under 3% of list.
5. **Honest, lender-ready numbers.** Year 1 shows a pre-tax profit of only $1,908 after $23,200 of one-time launch costs, then $35,339 and $49,819. Debt coverage is 1.40× in Year 1 (2.71× before one-time costs), 3.24× in Year 2, and 4.01× in Year 3. Break-even is about 14 visits a day, and cash never falls below $17,218 (April 2027).

## Why This Matters

- **The current page doesn't show a plan.** /template/nail-salon-business-plan-example lists what to include but never shows a finished salon plan with prices, staffing, or statements. Searchers looking for an example leave without one.
- **Published nail salon samples skip the hard parts.** One widely shown sample has no financial statements, no loan rate, and payroll as a single line. Another worked example contradicts its own station count and margin. None explains how techs are paid or how full the chairs need to be.
- **Tech pay is the decision first-time owners struggle with most.** Commission, hourly, or booth rental changes the whole cost structure. This plan picks commission and shows the per-hour pay, the minimum-wage floor, and what it means for margin.
- **Demand for nail care keeps growing.** The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects jobs for manicurists and pedicurists to grow 9% from 2025 to 2035, much faster than average ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/personal-care-and-service/manicurists-and-pedicurists.htm)).
- **Charlotte is the kind of market readers recognize.** It added more residents than any other U.S. city between July 2024 and July 2025, reaching 964,784 ([Charlotte Regional Business Alliance](https://charlotteregion.com/news/charlotte-nears-population-of-1-million/)). A salon plan set in a real, growing city reads like one a lender would actually see.
