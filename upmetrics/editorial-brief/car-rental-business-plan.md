## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> Tollbrook Car Rental seeks a $130,000 SBA 7(a) loan to launch a 10-vehicle off-airport fleet in Carrow Point, North Carolina. The plan shows that each vehicle must earn about $1,408 a month (19.8 paid days) to cover every cost, including depreciation and loan interest. It shows that 10 vehicles is the smallest fleet that covers the owner's salary and the loan. And it shows Tollbrook keeps paying the bank even if bookings run 10% slow.

### Key Messages

1. **Revenue is built from paid rental days, not fleet size.** 10 vehicles have 3,650 available days a year. Tollbrook plans to fill 2,200 of them in Year 1 (60.3%), 2,420 in Year 2 (66.3%) and 2,662 in Year 3 (72.9%). Rates are held flat for three years, so all growth comes from more paid days.
2. **Every vehicle has a monthly bill to beat.** Depreciation, insurance, maintenance, interest and a share of overhead come to $1,196 per vehicle per month. At a $60.31 contribution per paid day, that's 19.8 paid days, or $1,408 in revenue, per vehicle per month.
3. **Screening, deposits and insurance protect the fleet.** Every renter passes a license, ID and driving-record check, a card deposit hold, and a coverage check before getting keys. GPS telematics in every car cut theft and non-return risk.
4. **Downtime is planned, not ignored.** The utilization targets leave room for about 12 days per vehicle per year for maintenance, repairs, recalls and detailing. The stress test shows what one $4,800 repair does to cash.
5. **The owner and the bank get paid first.** Trevor's salary sits in operating expenses. Debt service coverage (EBITDA ÷ loan payments) is 1.51× in Year 2 and 1.81× in Year 3. The $69,456 of cash at the end of Year 3 funds the first round of vehicle replacements.

- The current page at this URL dates from March 2023 and needs a full refresh. Searchers for "car rental business plan" want a filled-in example with numbers they can check.

### What to Borrow / What to Avoid

**Borrow:**
- Reference 1's monthly ramp and the "size working capital from the lowest month" logic. We show Tollbrook's Year 1 month-by-month cash, with the low point ($19,687 in month 2) sitting just under the $21,000 reserve.
- Reference 1's "add one car" calculation. We turn it into the "What each vehicle must earn" table and the rule for when to add a vehicle.
- Reference 2's buy-don't-lease point and its add-on revenue list. Both shape Tollbrook's fleet plan and its rate card.
- Reference 3's chapter order, with a dedicated fleet chapter between Market and Operations.

**Avoid:**
- Reference 1's missing owner pay, depreciation and replacement. Tollbrook's plan includes all three, which is why its net income looks thinner than its cash flow.
- Reference 1's utilization counted against every calendar day with no downtime. Tollbrook reserves about 12 days per vehicle per year for maintenance and repairs.
- Reference 2's silence on screening, deposits and coverage. Tollbrook's Operations chapter makes them explicit.
- How-to explanations in general. This is a finished plan written in the third person, not a tutorial.
