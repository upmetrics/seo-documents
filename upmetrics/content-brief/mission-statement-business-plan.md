## 2. Why We'll Win

- **We answer the placement question no one else does.** Four of five competitors never say where the mission statement goes in a business plan; PrometAI says only that it is "the foundation of a business plan." The SBA explicitly lists it inside the **executive summary**. Quoting a .gov source on the exact question the keyword implies is our strongest single differentiator.
- **We're the only ones telling readers what to leave out.** Zero competitors explain what belongs in other business plan sections. This is precisely why first-time writers cram their value proposition, target market, and five-year goals into one sentence and end up with something generic. Naming the exclusions is more useful than naming the inclusions.
- **Realistic small-business examples instead of Fortune 500 wallpaper.** Between them, competitors use Starbucks, Nike, Google, Tesla, Amazon, Coca-Cola, Patagonia, Slack, Asana, LinkedIn, and Microsoft. Someone writing a plan for a cleaning company can't model anything on those. We write six small-business examples and pair each with a weak version showing the failure mode.
- **A pass/fail final check, not adjectives.** The closest any competitor comes is OnStrategy's "foundational, original, memorable" — adjectives, not a test. We ship a 5-question checklist anchored on the swap test: cover your business name; could this be any competitor?
- **One running example built start to finish.** LivePlan gives eight loosely ordered tips and Stripe gives four abstract principles, and neither ever produces a finished statement. We carry a single business through all four steps so the reader watches a statement get built, then does the same on their own line.

**Supplementary research substitute** (used in place of UGC, lower confidence — verify before quoting):

**Recurring failure patterns in small-business mission statements:**
1. **The swap test failure** — per [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/5-reasons-why-your-mission-statement-probably-stinks/219544), if you can swap your company name for a competitor's and the statement still reads true, it says nothing. This is the sharpest single test found in any source and should anchor our final-check section.
2. **Buzzword padding** — "synergistic paradigms," "leveraging core competencies," "value-added frameworks," "world-class," "best possible," "great customer service." LivePlan makes the same point: these phrases mean little because everyone uses them.
3. **Overlength** — a widely repeated heuristic holds that if a mission statement takes more than 10 seconds to read, it's too long.
4. **Placement confusion is real and documented** — [Accion](https://us.accion.org/resource/business-plan-section-1-executive-summary/) notes the statement "might fit in the executive summary, or you can include it in the company description section instead." That ambiguity is exactly what leaves readers stuck. Our job is to give the default (executive summary, per SBA), the exception, and a rule for choosing — not to repeat the ambiguity.

**Language patterns to use:**
- Readers say "what my business does" — not "organizational purpose"
- Readers say "sounds generic" / "could be anyone" — not "lacks differentiation"
- Readers say "where do I put it" — not "placement within document architecture"

**How this shaped the outline:**
- H2 "Where the mission statement goes in your business plan" added because placement ambiguity is documented and no competitor resolves it
- H2 "The final check" built around the swap test
- H2 "Mission statement examples for small businesses" uses weak/strong pairs because buzzword padding is the dominant failure mode

## Appendix: Writing Tone Guide

**DO:**
- Write in second person, directly to someone with their plan open
- Use plain nouns and real business types: a cleaning company, a dental practice, a food truck
- Give numbers, not ranges, wherever a number is defensible
- Show the ugly first draft before the polished version
- Keep sentences short in instructional sections

**DON'T:**
- Reach for a Fortune 500 example. Not once. Not even as a contrast
- Write about brand identity, company culture, or employee motivation. Those are the branding-theory traps that made the current page fail
- Hedge the length rule or the placement rule with "it depends"
- Pad the final-check section with encouragement — it is a checklist

**Banned AI terms (do not use anywhere):** unlock, unleash, harness, leverage (verb), elevate, delve, dive into, deep dive, navigate (metaphorical), landscape, realm, tapestry, beacon, game-changer, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, next-level, seamless(ly), robust, holistic, empower, supercharge, revolutionize, transform (hyperbolic), journey (metaphorical), bustling, thriving, vibrant, comprehensive guide, ultimate guide, definitive guide (in body copy), moreover, furthermore, additionally, in essence, foster, facilitate, streamline (filler), myriad, plethora, multifaceted, pivotal, paramount, indispensable, crucial/essential (empty emphasis), firstly/secondly/lastly, ensuring, utilizing, noteworthy, remarkably, undoubtedly, rest assured, with that said, key takeaways, actionable insights, in today's [X] world, it's important to note, it's worth mentioning, when it comes to, in the world of, look no further, whether you're a...or a..., not just...but also, it goes without saying, take [X] to the next level, tailored to your needs, in conclusion, to sum up
