## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> Penhallow Paper Co. is asking for an $85,000 SBA 7(a) loan to open a 1,100 sq ft stationery shop on Mill Street. The plan works because it is built on the numbers that decide whether a small paper shop survives (sales a day, average purchase, margin by product line, how fast stock sells and how much cash sits on the shelves), and those numbers cover the loan payments 1.34 times in Year 1 (before one-time opening costs) and 1.97 times by Year 3.

### Key Messages

1. **The forecast is a count of sales, not a market-share guess.** 12,500 transactions at $28 in Year 1 means about 41 sales a trading day. About 310 regular enthusiasts visiting monthly supply 30% of those transactions; gift shoppers, students and downtown workers supply the rest.
2. **Margin is set product by product.** Greeting cards cost 44% of their shelf price; pens and planners cost 55%; personalization costs 60%. The mix blends to a 50% merchandise cost, including freight and a 1.5% shrink allowance.
3. **Inventory is the biggest cash decision in the business.** $44,000 of opening stock is 35% of the startup budget. The plan targets about 3.5 turns a year (104 days of stock), plans holiday buying by quarter, and stages October deliveries so cash never runs out during the build-up.
4. **The owner and the help are paid in full.** Owen's $45,000 salary and about 2,370 hours of associate time at $15.50 an hour are in the numbers before any profit or loan payment.
5. **The loan is sized to what it buys.** Stock, fixtures, a modest build-out and a working capital reserve total $125,000. Year 1 shows a $7,603 pre-tax loss only because of $10,900 in one-time opening costs paid from startup capital; cash ends every year above the opening $29,300.

- The current page tells readers how to write a stationery plan but never shows a finished one. A complete, lender-ready example matches the other new-format samples in the library.

- Almost no sample plan explains inventory turns, open-to-buy or the cash squeeze before the holidays, even though that is where small gift and paper shops get into trouble. The quarterly stock and cash tables make this plan useful beyond the fictional business.
- It shows Upmetrics' financial forecasting at work: a transaction-based revenue forecast, monthly seasonality, a margin build-up by product line, inventory and payables, owner pay and a full SBA loan schedule.
