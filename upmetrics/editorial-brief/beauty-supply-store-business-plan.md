## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> A neighborhood beauty supply store succeeds on what it keeps in stock: the right braiding hair colors, wig styles and hair care for the people who live nearby, bought on good supplier terms and reordered before they run out. Crownwell Beauty Supply shows how a first-time owner with nine years of beauty retail behind her turns $235,000 into a well-stocked East Oak Cliff store that covers its loan payments 1.61 times in Year 2.

### Key Messages

1. **Stock is the business.** $85,000 of opening inventory (about 6,550 products) is the largest use of the money, and $42,483 of working capital pays for restocking while new distributor accounts pay on delivery.
2. **Stock follows the neighborhood.** East Oak Cliff is about half Black and half Hispanic, with a median household income of $34,360. Crownwell's shelves lead with braiding hair, wigs and textured hair care, carry the hair color and skincare Hispanic shoppers buy, keep prices close to big-box stores on everyday items, and have Spanish speakers on every shift.
3. **Margin comes from hair, not shampoo.** Hair care is 30% of sales at a 34% margin; wigs, braiding hair and tools carry 52–55%. Pricing everyday staples competitively brings people in; hair and accessories pay the rent.
4. **Suppliers and reorder rules are written down.** Five distributor and hair-company accounts, pay-on-delivery for three months then Net 30, case buying for braiding hair in matching dye lots, and min/max reorder levels in the POS for every product.
5. **The numbers are conservative.** Revenue $648,000 → $712,800 → $784,080 on about 66 sales a day in Year 1; a −$9,420 pre-tax loss in Year 1 (after $19,550 of one-time costs), then $18,947 and $29,036. Cash never falls below $31,840.

- **The current page has no plan in it.** The 2021 page at this URL lists the chapters a plan should have. People searching "beauty supply store business plan" want to see one written out, with startup costs and inventory numbers they can compare with their own.

### What to Borrow / What to Avoid

**Borrow:**
- Reference 1's chapter set as a checklist, and its loyalty-points idea, turned into Crownwell's loyalty program and Pro Card.
- Reference 2's category list and its points on shade depth and expiration dates, used in Products & Pricing and Inventory Control. Its 45-sales-a-day × $24 model is a useful sense check: Crownwell's 66 a day at $27 sits above it for a larger store.
- Reference 3's sourcing rules (sample first, written tier pricing, dye lots, return terms, few reliable suppliers, monthly sell-through review), which become Crownwell's Suppliers & Ordering and Inventory Control sections with real numbers.

**Avoid:**
- Startup ranges and blended margins without a worked model (all three). Every Crownwell figure is one number that ties to the statements.
- Financial tables that contradict each other (Reference 1).
- Out-of-date or unsourced industry figures, and promotional calls to action inside the plan.
- Second-person advice inside the plan. The plan is written about Crownwell.

### Writing Tone

**For Sample Business Plan:**
- Plain, practical and confident, written by a first-time owner who has run a beauty supply store for someone else and knows the products. Use "Crownwell," "we," "Kiara," "the store" or "the team," whichever reads most naturally; mix the subjects. The introduction speaks as "we" (Upmetrics), never "I."
- Open each section with what it does for the customer or the business. Follow each fact with what it means for Crownwell ("This means…", "This helps…"). One number per sentence outside the market data and the financial tables.

**Q2:** Is a beauty supply store profitable?
**A2:** Explain that it can be once daily sales cover the fixed costs and the loan. In this sample, break-even is about 55 sales a day at a $27 average sale, and the store plans about 73 a day in Year 2. Explain how to work it out: add up yearly fixed costs (rent, owner pay, insurance, marketing, utilities), then divide by what each sale leaves after product cost, store labor and card fees ($7.09 here). Add that margins differ a lot by category: everyday hair care earns about a third of the price, while wigs and braiding hair earn about half, so the category mix matters as much as sales.
**Source:** Section 6B (break-even, category mix) + editorial judgment
**Why not in main content:** The plan gives Crownwell's numbers; this explains the method for readers.

**Q3:** What licenses do you need to open a beauty supply store, and how do you find wholesale suppliers?
