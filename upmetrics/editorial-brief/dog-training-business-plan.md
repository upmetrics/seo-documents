## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> Heelcroft Dog Training is asking for a $40,000 SBA Microloan to launch a certified trainer's in-home and group-class business in Wrenhollow, Ohio. The plan rests on one constraint: a trainer sells hours. Private lessons fill Renata Calloway's 32 client-facing hours a week first and keep the most per hour; group classes in rented rooms are how Heelcroft grows past her calendar, because an associate can teach them and they move families from one program to the next.

### Key Messages

1. **Trainer hours are the inventory.** Renata Calloway has 1,472 client-facing hours a year (32 a week × 46 weeks, including drive time). Year 1 uses 82% of them, which is why the plan hires a part-time associate trainer at the start of Year 2. Her utilization falls to 66% by Year 3 while revenue grows 56%.
2. **Private lessons pay the bills early.** Private packages, single lessons, and behavior coaching make up 64% of Year 1 list revenue. After drive time and direct costs, an hour of private work keeps about $86–$88, against $54 for a puppy class and $78 for Family Manners.
3. **Group classes carry the growth because they can be staffed.** A Family Manners course taught by the associate in Year 2 brings in $1,254 and keeps $470 after the room, the class assistant, the associate's pay, supplies, and payroll tax, without using any of the owner's hours. Group seats rise from 54% to 66% of enrollments by Year 3.
4. **Families move through a path, and they do the practice.** Puppy Foundations leads to Family Manners (35% target), then Out & About or a private tune-up (25% and 20%); returning clients rise from about 20% of enrollments in Year 1 to about 30% by Year 3. Every program requires the dog's owner to attend and practice between sessions, with short homework videos after each lesson.
5. **Clear limits and honest numbers.** Heelcroft handles everyday manners and basic behavior problems. Dogs with a bite history, aggression toward people, or signs of clinical anxiety are referred to their veterinarian and to a veterinary behaviorist. Year 1 shows a pre-tax profit of only $823 after $11,300 of one-time launch costs, then $17,659 and $25,120. Debt coverage is 1.14× in Year 1 (2.39× before one-time costs), 2.94× in Year 2, and 3.70× in Year 3. Cash never falls below $11,784 (May 2027).

## Why This Matters

- **The Upmetrics library has five dog business samples and none for training.** Dog walking, grooming, kennel, daycare, and breeding are covered. Training is the one service that owners of all those dogs need, and the new plan links naturally to each existing sample.
- **More households own dogs, and owners are doing more themselves.** Dog ownership reached 71 million U.S. households (53%) in 2025, up 4% in a year, and owners are bringing more care into the home ([APPA, July 2026](https://americanpetproducts.org/news/americas-dog-owners-are-becoming-smarter-spenders)). That favors training that teaches the family, not a dog sent away.
- **Published dog training plans don't hold up.** One widely shown sample builds its revenue on advertising and blogging services, with a balance sheet that doesn't balance. Another gives no prices, capacity, or wages. None shows how many hours a trainer has or what each program earns per hour.
- **The real questions are about time.** Founders want to know how many clients one trainer can carry, whether to start with private lessons or classes, when to hire, and what a rented room costs per class. This plan answers each with one set of numbers.
- **Adopters are a large, steady market.** About 2 million dogs were adopted from U.S. shelters and rescues in 2025, with more adoptions in spring and summer ([Shelter Animals Count](https://www.shelteranimalscount.org/2025-report/)). Many arrive with no training history, which is the gap Heelcroft's adopter credit and Family Manners class are built for.
