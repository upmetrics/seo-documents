### Top 3 Problems

1. **Google dropped the page from its index.** URL Inspection reports "Crawled – currently not indexed" since the 2026-06-20 crawl, and impressions have been 0 since 2026-06-23. Nothing can rank until it is re-indexed.
2. **The content isn't differentiated enough to earn a place in the index.** Its taxonomy mixes data sources, data types and purposes into one list. It has no at-a-glance types table and no strengths/limitations per type. It's missing descriptive/causal research and the types-vs-methods distinction that the PAA questions ask about. Content quality found 12 filler, 10 vague and 16 readability issues.
3. **The page is nearly orphaned.** The only referring URL Google knows is `/sitemap`, and no related blog post links to it in context, so Google has little reason to prioritize it.

## 4. SERP & Intent Analysis

**What Google is rewarding:**
- **Dominant intent:** Informational ("what are the types").
- **Content types ranking:** 6 of 8 organic results are educational guides or listicles (Qualtrics, GWI, Valona, The Hartford, 2-Europe, Crunchbase). The rest are a glossary page (LexisNexis), a SlideShare deck and an Investopedia definition.
- **What this means:** Google wants a clearly structured taxonomy of research types, each with a definition, its methods and an example. Most results are only 435–2,900 words. Links don't decide this SERP: KD is 11 and the ranking pages have 0–52 referring domains.
- **How results frame the count:** "8 types" (Qualtrics, 2-Europe), "4 main types" (Valona), "primary vs secondary" (The Hartford), "+10 methods" (Crunchbase). The common structure is **primary vs secondary, then qualitative vs quantitative, then goal-based types (brand, competitor, customer, product, pricing, segmentation, ad/campaign testing)**, with methods (surveys, interviews, focus groups, observation, experiments) treated as a separate layer.

**Intent match/mismatch:**
- **Current page type:** Blog article / guide
- **Match status:** ⚠️ Partial mismatch
- **Why:** The page format matches the SERP, but its taxonomy mixes axes. It lists "Primary, Secondary, Qualitative, Quantitative, Exploratory" as five peer "types", then adds five "specialized" types. Ranking pages separate *data source* (primary/secondary), *data type* (qual/quant) and *purpose* (exploratory/descriptive/causal plus business-goal types) into distinct layers. The page also has no quick-scan summary table, which the top results and the AI Overview rely on.
