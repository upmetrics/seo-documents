## 2. Why We'll Win

- **No one has analyzed this deck.** Eight of the nine organic results either host the PDF or sell a template. The single genuine analysis ([alexanderjarvis.com](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/tinder-pitch-deck-seed-stage-startup/), position 6) is 280 words with two bullet lists, no dates, no sources, and broken image placeholders. A real slide-by-slide read is unclaimed territory.
- **The most interesting story in this deck is one nobody tells.** Slide 10 pitches in-app purchases — $1 to see each new match after the first two, paid virtual gifts, paid placement in the "Like or Not" list. Tinder shipped none of it. It stayed free for roughly three years before Tinder Plus launched. A deck whose revenue slide was completely wrong still worked. That contrast is the article's spine and no competitor touches it.
- **The deck breaks the rules and won anyway.** There is no team slide, no market sizing, no competitor grid, no traction numbers, no financials, and no ask. Every "what belongs in a pitch deck" checklist online says that deck should have failed. Explaining why it didn't is more useful than grading it — and it lets us link naturally to our own team-slide and competition-slide guides.
- **Zero competitors have an FAQ section.** All five scraped pages: none. Two relevant PAA questions are sitting unclaimed ("What is a pitch deck for a dating app?" and "What is the 10/20/30 rule for pitch deck?").
- **Series authority plus DR 74 against KD 1.** Fourteen sibling pages already rank for `[company] pitch deck` — OpenAI at position 1.5, Stripe at 4.5, DoorDash at 4.7. The hardest competing domain that actually analyzes anything is DR 70 with a 280-word page.
