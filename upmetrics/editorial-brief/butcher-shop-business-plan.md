## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> Coleman Meat Market is asking for a $205,000 SBA 7(a) loan to open a full-service butcher shop run by a 17-year meat cutter. The plan works because it is built on the numbers that decide a butcher shop's survival (how much saleable meat each case yields, how trim is sold instead of thrown away, how little spoils, what skilled labor costs and what the owner is paid), and those numbers cover the loan payments 1.28 times in Year 1 and 2.32 times by Year 3.

### Key Messages

1. **The forecast is a count of customers, not a market-share guess.** 19,375 transactions at $44 in Year 1 means about 63 sales a trading day, or roughly 1,050 regular households visiting 1.25 times a month. That is about 2.5% of the 42,000 households within three miles.
2. **Margin is made in the cutting room.** A case of strip loins bought at $9.40 a pound really costs $10.68 a pound once fat and cutting loss come off. Selling the trim as ground beef and sausage is what keeps meat cost at 55% of sales.
3. **Shrink is planned, not hoped away.** The plan reserves 2.5% of purchases for moisture loss, end cuts, markdowns and spoilage, a figure Andre beat at his last counter (2.2%), and orders twice a week to keep only 10 days of stock.
4. **Skilled labor and the owner are paid in full.** A journeyman butcher at $25/hour, an apprentice at $18.50 and Andre's own $62,400 salary are all in the numbers before any profit or loan payment.
5. **The loan is sized to what it buys and repaid from operations.** Premises work, refrigeration, cutting equipment, opening stock and working capital total $260,000. Year 1 shows a pre-tax loss of $19,373 only because of $19,000 in one-time opening costs, and year-end cash stays above the opening $55,460 in every year.

### What to Borrow / What to Avoid

**Borrow:**
- Reference 1's one-page executive summary with a funding box, its honest supermarket-competition framing and its "before opening / first two months / first year" milestone list
- Reference 1's per-unit thinking, rebuilt properly: per-transaction revenue plus a per-category meat cost after yield (see the Product Mix table)
- Reference 2's lender checklist: every assumption stated and defended, seasonal low months explained before the banker asks, loan amount, rate and term in one line, and the inspection type named (retail exemption)
- Reference 3's chapter coverage (industry, target market, pricing, startup budget, milestones) as a completeness check only

**Avoid:**
- Gross margins with no yield, trim or shrink behind them (References 1 and 3)
- Revenue that grows without transactions, price and visit frequency to explain it (Reference 3)
- Funding totals and percentages that don't match (Reference 3)
- Wholesale, abattoir, franchising or game-processing extras that the business doesn't offer
- Unverified national statistics: use the verified external links in Section 6 or none at all
