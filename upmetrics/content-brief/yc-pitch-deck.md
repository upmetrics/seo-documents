## 2. Why We'll Win

- **Every competitor gets the slide count wrong, and we can prove it.** Published guides claim "7 slides according to YC" (Forbes), "9 slides according to YCombinator" (DesignLed), "10 slides" (Leland), and "10-12 slides" (several). They are all quoting different YC artifacts as if they were one. YC actually publishes **three separate deck formats**: a Demo Day deck (5-7 slides, 2 min 30 sec), a seed round deck (Aaron Harris's template), and a Series A deck (10-15 slides, 15-20 min). No ranking page separates them. **This is the single biggest gap on the SERP.**
- **We name the actual authors and quote them verbatim.** Not one competitor cites Aaron Harris, Janelle Tam, or Kevin Hale by name — they paraphrase YC anonymously and invent framework names YC never used (Slidebean's "Secret Sauce" slide; Leland's "P.A.I.N. framework"). We quote the real rules: *"Nobody cares about your advisors,"* the "fundraising vertebrae" exercise, the n≤3 slide-set rule, and the "trick slide."
- **We have three real YC company decks already analyzed — nobody else does.** Stripe (YC S09), DoorDash (YC S13), and Dropbox (YC S07) already have full slide-by-slide breakdowns on upmetrics.co. Leland cites zero real YC decks; Ink Narrates cites two non-YC companies (Brex, Front). We turn the abstract template into proof.
- **The SERP is unusually soft.** Outside ycombinator.com itself, the competition is a LinkedIn post, a Reddit thread, a template download page, a **DR 18** blog, and a library *search results page*. Upmetrics is DR 74. There is no purpose-built article to beat here — only a vacuum to fill.
- **An AI Overview is already present.** Structuring each answer as a direct, quotable, sourced statement targets AI Overview citation, not just blue-link ranking.
