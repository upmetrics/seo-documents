## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> Treetop Play Loft is asking for a $320,000 SBA 7(a) loan to open a 6,000 sq ft indoor playground and play café in Harlow Springs, Tennessee. The plan rests on one idea: walk-in play fills the room, but birthday parties and memberships, booked and billed in advance, pay the rent.

### Key Messages

1. **Predictable revenue comes first.** Parties ($225,000) and memberships ($117,000) make up 45.6% of Year 1 revenue. Both are booked or billed ahead of time, so rent, core staffing, and loan payments are planned against them — not against weather-dependent walk-ins.
2. **The room is designed around the party calendar.** Three party rooms and 27 weekly party slots give Treetop room to grow from ~10 parties a week in Year 1 without adding space.
3. **The numbers are honest.** Year 1 shows a $25,588 pre-tax loss because it absorbs $32,000 of one-time opening costs. The business turns a profit from Year 2 ($44,933), and debt service coverage reaches 2.14× in Year 2.
4. **The margin for error is visible.** Break-even is 25,458 child visits a year (~70 a day). The Year 1 forecast of 30,000 visits (~82 a day) sits 18% above it.
5. **Safety and cleanliness are a selling point, not a footnote.** A ball-free design, ASTM F1918-certified equipment, and a posted cleaning schedule answer the first question parents ask.

### What to Borrow / What to Avoid

**Borrow:**
- Reference 1's operational detail: hours by day, staff counts by shift, and break-even stated in both dollars and visits.
- Reference 1's revenue split by stream — we go further by tying each stream to a driver (visits × ticket, parties × average package).
- Reference 2's separate targeting of toddler parents, schools, and daycares, and its split of start-up expenses vs. start-up assets.
- Reference 3's framing of multiple revenue streams and its stand-alone safety and compliance chapter.

**Avoid:**
- Reference 1's length — our plan should be half as long with the same completeness.
- Reference 2's placeholder names and generic objectives — every goal in our plan has a number and a date.
- Reference 3's second-person advice tone inside the plan chapters.
- Dated pricing and unsourced market claims (all three references do this somewhere).
