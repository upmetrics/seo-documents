**What Probelane does (five parts of one app; describe each individually in the Product chapter):**
1. **Temperature & cooling logs** — staff record cooler, freezer, hot-holding and cooking temperatures by tapping a reading in, or by touching an optional Bluetooth probe thermometer to the food. Cooling logs track two-stage cooling against the Food Code limits. Every entry carries a name and a timestamp.
2. **Opening & closing checklists** — a restaurant's own daily, weekly and monthly lists (sanitizer strength, handwashing sinks, date labels, pest checks), built from Probelane templates in about 30 minutes.
3. **Corrective actions & manager alerts** (Pro and Group) — a reading out of range opens a task ("discard or reheat; record what you did"), and a missed check sends the manager a text message.
4. **Inspection-ready reports & rule templates** — one-tap PDF of any date range for a health inspector, plus templates for the 2026 Tennessee rules: written allergen notices for unpackaged food, a written vomit and diarrhea cleanup procedure, and cooking-temperature checks at the new 155°F-for-17-seconds and 165°F-instantaneous standards. Essentials gets the templates and a 30-day report; Pro and Group get full history and scheduled weekly reports.
5. **Group dashboard** (Group tier) — one screen showing every location's completion rate, missed checks and open corrective actions, plus a weekly roll-up email to the owner.

## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> A SaaS business plan holds up when every number traces back to one waterfall, paying customers at the start plus new ones minus the ones who leave, and Probelane shows how that waterfall sets MRR, costs, cash, and the size of the raise for a small, non-venture-backed software company.

### Key Messages

1. **Churn is a line in the plan, not a footnote.** Probelane loses 26, 77, and 117 locations in Years 1–3 because restaurants close. By Year 3, about a third of new sales only replace closures.
2. **Payroll is the real startup cost of SaaS.** The product is already built; 90% of the $400,000 raised is a cash reserve that carries salaries while MRR grows, and payroll is 71% of Year 2 costs.
3. **Unit economics decide whether debt is safe.** CAC is $452–$617 with founder selling time included; payback is under 9 months every year, so each new location repays its cost inside a year.
4. **The raise is sized from the cash low point, with a downside plan.** Cash bottoms at $62,005 in June 2029, then monthly cash flow turns positive. If Years 2–3 sales run 20% slow, two named cost moves keep the low point above $100,000.
5. **You don't need venture capital to fund a small SaaS business.** Founders' savings, four customers who became angels, and a CDFI loan with interest-only first year fund it, and the founders keep 84%.
