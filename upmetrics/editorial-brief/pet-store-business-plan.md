## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> Bark & Burrow Pet Supply is asking for a $240,000 SBA 7(a) loan to open a 3,000 sq ft neighborhood pet supply store in Harrow Park, Ohio. The plan rests on one idea: food and litter bring a pet owner back every few weeks, so the store competes hardest for that repeat purchase — with advice, same-day delivery, and frequent-buyer rewards — and earns its margin on the treats, toys, and supplies added to the same basket.

### Key Messages

1. **The repeat purchase is the business.** Food and litter are 56% of sales at thin margins (30–36%), but they bring about 1,400 regular customers back roughly every 4 weeks. Treats, toys, supplements, and small-animal supplies (44% of sales) earn 50–60% margins on those same visits. Blended product margin: 44%.
2. **The store competes on what online and big-box sellers don't offer locally.** Nutrition advice from trained staff, a food-switch guarantee, free same-day delivery on orders of $49 or more within 7 miles, and Repeat Delivery on a schedule the customer picks.
3. **No live animals, more community.** Bark & Burrow sells no puppies, kittens, or other live animals. Monthly adoption events with two rescue partners bring new pet owners into the store on the day they need everything.
4. **The numbers are honest.** Year 1 shows a $12,431 pre-tax loss because it absorbs $16,000 of one-time opening costs. The store turns a profit from Year 2 ($22,378) and reaches $38,536 in Year 3. Debt service coverage is 1.61× in Year 2.
5. **The margin for error is visible.** Break-even is 14,530 transactions a year (~40 a day). The Year 1 forecast of 18,000 (~50 a day) sits 24% above it, and year-end cash never falls below $116,136.

## Why This Matters

- **Upmetrics has no retail pet plan.** The pet cluster has daycare, kennel, and grooming samples, but nothing for the most common pet business people search for: the neighborhood pet store.
- **Pet product spending is large and steady.** US pet owners spent $158 billion in 2025, including $68.3 billion on food and treats and $34.4 billion on supplies, live animals, and OTC medicine (APPA). Food is a habit purchase, which is what makes a small store fundable.
- **Published samples are thin or inconsistent.** The pages that show up for "pet store business plan" either hide the numbers in images or PDFs, use ranges that don't add up, or contradict themselves on startup costs and staffing. None shows statements that tie out.
- **No sample answers the real question.** Founders want to know how a 3,000 sq ft independent store survives next to Chewy autoship and a big-box chain six miles away. This plan answers it with a category margin mix, a delivery program, and a break-even stated in transactions per day.
- **The no-live-animals model matches where the industry is going.** A growing number of cities and states restrict retail puppy and kitten sales, and rescue partnerships are now a standard independent-store practice.
