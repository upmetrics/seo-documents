## 2. Why We'll Win

- **Two of the six pages ranking for this keyword aren't about sales at all.** The Hartford (position 5) and Rasmussen (position 4) are marketing articles — The Hartford's 13 tips cover SEO, email, social, reviews, and retargeting with *zero* coverage of prospecting, pitching, follow-up, or closing. Google is padding this SERP because genuinely good small-business *sales* content doesn't exist yet. We write the sales article the SERP is missing.
- **No competitor helps the reader choose.** All five scraped pages dump 7 to 18 strategies in a flat list and leave the owner to guess which apply to them. Our strategy selector table — business type × who does the selling × sales cycle → which 3 strategies to start with — is the single biggest gap in this SERP and our lead linkable asset.
- **Zero competitors publish a data table in the body.** Confirmed across all five scrapes: no comparison tables, no cost tables, no metric tables. Every audit flagged "no real numbers" as a weakness. We attach a cost, an effort level, and one metric to every strategy.
- **Nobody covers the price conversation.** Not one of the five pages addresses objection handling or how to defend a price — the two moments where small business deals actually die. This is core selling, and the entire first page ignores it.
- **The freshest-looking competitor is the stalest.** The Hartford ranks #5 with 8,807 monthly visits on stats that project forward to 2023 ("over 70% of the world's population will have mobile internet by 2023") and references Twitter, Snapchat, and Google My Business. RingCentral's only named case studies use 2018 revenue figures. Current-year data alone is a differentiator here.

## Appendix: Writing Tone Guide

**DO:**
- Write to one owner, not an audience. "You" throughout.
- Use real numbers. Every strategy needs a cost, a time commitment, and a metric.
- Use the reader's language: "crickets," "land a sale," "scrappy," "real conversations."
- Give the actual words to say in a sales or pricing conversation, in quotes.
- Commit to recommendations. Say which strategy to start with, not "it depends."

**DON'T:**
- Don't write about marketing. If a paragraph is about awareness, ads, or posting frequency, it belongs in a different article — link out instead.
- Don't assume a sales team, a CRM, or a budget.
- Don't pitch Upmetrics outside the forecasting tie-in. Teamgate's over-promotion is a weakness we're exploiting; don't repeat it.
- Don't recycle competitor statistics on faith. RingCentral's "5 follow-ups to close 80% of sales" and OnDeck's "75% never click past page one" are both thinly sourced.

**Use instead:** plain verbs, specific nouns, real numbers, direct statements, conversational phrasing.
