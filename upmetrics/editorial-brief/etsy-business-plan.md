## 2. Thesis & Key Messages

### Thesis Statement

> A craft kit looks profitable on materials alone: a $58 needlepoint kit uses $21 of materials. After assembly, Etsy's fees, and free shipping, it leaves $18.96. GoodHour Craft Kits plans around that true margin, launches on an $18,000 microloan with no sales history, and tests demand in stages before it buys inventory in bulk, so the loan is repaid from kits it has proven it can sell.

### Key Messages

1. **The true margin, not the materials margin.** A $58 beginner kit keeps $18.96 (32.7%) after materials, 16 minutes of paid assembly, the shipping label, and every Etsy fee. A $38 themed kit keeps $12.22. A $189 craft-night bundle keeps $88.09, which is why bundles are 10% of kits but 29% of revenue.
2. **A clear monthly target.** At about $23.33 of contribution per kit, GoodHour needs about 88 kits a month in 2028 to cover every fixed cost and the $338.02 loan payment. It plans 100.
3. **Inventory follows proof.** A 75-kit test batch comes first; the full catalog, the bundle, and the holiday buy each wait for a pass/fail check. Opening components cost $3,000, not a warehouse of finished kits.
4. **Supply and hands set the holiday ceiling.** Canvases take about three weeks to print, single thread colors can be backordered for up to six weeks, and October–December holds 52% of the year's kits. Staged orders, reorder points, and Owen's hours keep every holiday order shipping, and month-end cash never falls below $4,480.
5. **Honest about the risk.** No sales history means the forecast is a target. If sales come in 25% short, Jenna cuts her draw and ad spend, and the loan is still paid every month (Year 2 coverage 1.82×).

### What to Borrow / What to Avoid

**Borrow:**
- Reference 1's full chapter set and launch checklist, rebuilt with real numbers: GoodHour's milestones become pass/fail gates with dates and thresholds.
- Reference 2's "work back from a goal" math and fee stack, taken to the end: price → true contribution per kit → kits a month to cover fixed costs and the loan (Supporting Tables B and C).
- Reference 3's kit anatomy and "keep kits alike" advice: every GoodHour beginner kit uses the same canvas size, bar length, pouch, and box, which is what keeps assembly at 16 minutes and the reorder list short.

**Avoid:**
- Big round forecasts with nothing behind them, and budgets that don't add up (Reference 1).
- Margin math that stops before Etsy's fees and shipping (Reference 2's example).
- Retail pricing rules (cost × 4) presented as the answer: on Etsy, fees and free shipping take about a third of a beginner kit's price, so GoodHour prices from contribution, not from a multiple.
- Second-person advice and "how to open an Etsy shop" steps inside the plan.

**A2:** List the main U.S. fees in plain words: $0.20 to list (renewed when an item sells), 6.5% of the item price plus shipping, payment processing of 3% + $0.25, and Offsite Ads of 12–15% on orders that come from Etsy's outside ads (mandatory at 12% once a shop passes $10,000 a year). **Q3:** Can you get a loan to start an Etsy business with no sales?
**A3:** Say yes, but it's harder: lenders want a clear use of the money, a realistic plan, the owner's personal credit, and often a personal guarantee. **Source:** SBA microloan program facts + editorial judgment

**Q4:** How do I adapt this Etsy business plan for my own shop?
**A4:** Tell readers to change five inputs first: their own prices, the material and packaging cost of each product, the minutes it takes to make or assemble one, their shipping label cost, and how much they need to pay themselves.
